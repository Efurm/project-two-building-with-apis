Question 2 - Explain one prototype decision. What design question did your prototype address and why? Describe the alternatives you explored, the decision you made after using it, and where that decision appears in your completed application. My prototype answered the design question of how to rate a movie because using the prototype allowed me to view multiple ways of rating a movie. There were alternatives such as numbers with decimals on a scale of 1-5, just numbers on a scale of 1-5 and stars with halves allowed on a scale of 1-10. I made the decision to use whole stars only on a scale of 1-5 and it appears in library/models.py where rating is a PositiveSmallIntegerField with a min of 1 and a max of 5 for the rest of the application to pull from.

Question 3 - Trace your API update. Choose the update performed by your second script. Trace the request from the script through your Django URL, view, and serializer to the stored record and response. In your response, identify which URL pattern routes the request, which view handles it, and which serializer validates the data. Explain how Django identifies the user, how access is restricted, and how you verified that the change was saved. The script's rewatch with friends command first uses GET /api/entries/12/ to get the before state, where it then sends PATCH /api/entries/12/ with {"notes": "Rewatch with friends"} and an authorization token with the key header. In library/api/urls.py, the DRF DefaultRouter routes it to EntryViewSet.partial_update. Django then identifies the user using TokenAuthentication, and then sets request.user. Access is restricted by IsAuthenticated and by get_queryset(). EntrySerializer checks the fields and then runs Entry.clean(), then save() updates the SQLite row and the view returns 200 JSON. The save was verified by using runserver and tests that read the row from the database, since the script's after output comes from the PATCH response.
